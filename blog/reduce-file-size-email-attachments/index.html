How to Reduce File Size for Email Attachments

Trying to email a document or a few photos, only to see “attachment too large”? Resize large photos, optimise image-heavy PDFs, or use a sharing link for files that need to stay at full quality.

Start with a copy of your original file. Then reduce its size, check that the contents are still clear, and attach the smaller version.

Check the attachment limit first

Before compressing anything, check your email service’s attachment limit. Your recipient’s email system may have a different limit, so leave some room below the maximum.

For example, Google’s attachment guidance lists a 25 MB combined attachment limit for personal Gmail accounts. Several smaller files count towards that total.

Check the size of everything you plan to attach. Reducing one photo won’t solve the problem if the remaining attachments are still too large.

How to make photos smaller for email

Photos straight from a camera or phone can contain more detail than someone needs for viewing on a screen.

  1. Make a copy. Keep the original for printing or future editing.
  2. Reduce the dimensions. For casual screen viewing, try a longest edge of around 1,600–2,000 pixels as a starting point.
  3. Choose a suitable format. JPEG is often practical for photographs. PNG can be useful for screenshots, sharp text, or transparency.
  4. Adjust quality gradually. Start with a moderate setting, then inspect the result.
  5. Check the saved file size. Repeat only if it is still too large.

Resizing reduces the number of pixels. Lossy compression discards some information to reduce file size, which can affect image quality. If your recipient needs the original for printing, detailed inspection, or editing, use a sharing link instead of reducing it heavily.

How to reduce a PDF attachment

Look for an option labelled Compress PDF, Optimise PDF, or Reduced Size PDF. PDF optimisation can reduce image resolution and recompress images, making it useful for scans and documents containing large photographs. Adobe’s PDF compression guide explains these techniques.

Use a moderate setting first. Open the smaller PDF and inspect small text, numbers, signatures, diagrams, and every page—including the last one.

If you still have the original Word document or presentation, try compressing its pictures and exporting a fresh PDF. Avoid converting clear text into screenshots when searchability or accessibility matters.

How to prepare an attachment with FileFit

  1. Open FileFit and choose Compress.
  2. Select Fit a size and enter your target in MB or KB.
  3. Choose your files. For photos, More options also lets you set a maximum image size.
  4. Download the results, open them, and check the combined size before attaching them.
Two details to remember

FileFit’s target applies per file, not to the whole email. Five files that each meet your target can still exceed the combined attachment limit.

FileFit’s PDF compression turns pages into images inside a new PDF, so text is no longer selectable. Keep your original, and use a method that preserves text if the recipient needs to search, copy, or access it with assistive technology.

Will a ZIP file help?

A ZIP archive packages several files into one attachment and may reduce their combined size. JPEG photos, MP4 videos, and many modern document formats already use compression, so zipping them may make little difference.

Compare the archive with the originals and use it only if it helps. The recipient will need to extract the files.

What if the file is still too large?

If the smaller version becomes blurry or difficult to read, stop reducing it. Upload the original to a file-sharing service and email a link. Check that your recipient has permission to open or download it.

You can also remove unnecessary pages or send separate, clearly labelled emails when that suits the recipient. For confidential material, choose a compression or sharing method appropriate to the information.

Your checklist before sending

  • Confirm the total attachment size.
  • Open the exact files you attached.
  • Check text, photos, and page order.
  • Use clear filenames, such as Estimate-email-copy.pdf.
  • Keep the originals.
  • Check access permissions if you are sending a link.

Frequently asked questions

Can I reduce file size without losing quality?

Sometimes. Lossless compression preserves the original data, but the reduction may be limited. Resizing and lossy compression can produce smaller files by removing detail or information.

Why did compression barely change my file?

The file may already be compressed. Try reducing image dimensions, optimising embedded pictures, or sharing the original through a link.

Should I compress the same photo repeatedly?

Return to the original when trying different settings. Repeatedly saving a photo with lossy compression can gradually reduce its quality.

Make your next attachment fit

Prepare a smaller copy, compare it with the original, and send the smallest version that still does the job.
